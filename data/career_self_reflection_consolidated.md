## Non-negotiables to take/keep/leave a job

1. **Autonomy + white space** — room to find the problem, not just execute someone else's solution
2. **A "Glue" mandate** — cross-functional collaboration must be the job, not a workaround
3. **Impact over busywork** — license to build systems and automate inefficiency; no manual repetition without the ability to fix it
4. **Meaningful over prestigious** — high performance in an unengaging role is the cautionary tale already lived
5. **Mentorship culture** — an environment that values developing people as much as driving revenue
6. **Calendar sovereignty** — losing control of time hits mood and output directly

## Biggest development areas

1. **Self-advocacy and visibility** — best work lives in systems and infrastructure people use without knowing who built them; need to learn to narrate it
2. **Scaling through others** — don't be the only one who knows how the glue is mixed; systems must outlast involvement
3. **Leaning into your own POV** — tendency to defer or go soft when there's actual conviction; the work is holding ground without it feeling like a fight
4. **Shipping before it's perfect** — over-anticipates feedback before anything is out there; fix is lowering the stakes and treating every post/output like a brick, not the whole house (ref: Bryan Collins 6.22.26 — "you don't scrutinize every brick in the house")
5. **Executive presence in ambiguity** — deciding with less data, faster, than natural style prefers

## Additional insight: cognitive operating system

When asked to describe how his mind works, Drew said (paraphrased, June 2026):

> Everything is checklists. Everything is structured hierarchy / taxonomy. Everything is program / project management. Everything is a flowchart.

This is not a quirk — it's the default rendering mode. Drew sees structure in things before anyone has named it. Most people who are good at systems thinking learned it; for Drew it appears to be more native. This is closer to what a taxonomist or information architect actually *is* than what most job descriptions for those roles describe.

**Shadow side worth watching:** structure can be a way of managing uncertainty. Some things (career decisions, creative ideas, relationships) resist being structured until you've sat with the mess longer than feels comfortable. The checklist impulse can short-circuit that.
